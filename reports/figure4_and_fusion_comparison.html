MolPAL Figure 4 reproduction & a frozen-representation fusion model, on Enamine HTS (2.1M)

Reproducing Graff, Shakhnovich & Coley (2021) Figure 4 against thymidylate kinase (4UNN), and comparing three active-learning strategies — MolPAL+MPN, MolPAL+MoLFormer (fine-tuned in-loop), and a frozen three-backbone fusion model — under greedy and UCB acquisition.

Pool2,141,515 molecules OracleAutoDock Vina vs. 4UNN Metricfraction of true top-1000 found Repoal-molecular Date2026-08-10
All 30 configured active-learning runs completed successfully (18 for the three-method comparison, 6 for the Figure 4 reproduction, 3 for the fine-tuned-fusion follow-up, 3 for the learned-fusion follow-up), after tracing and fixing eight independent bugs in the vendored MolPAL/chemprop stack — four genuine out-of-memory risks (one unbounded cache measured at ~15.3 TB, one pre-existing default at ~1.77 TB, one from a SLURM per-CPU memory cap silently overriding --exclusive, one eager whole-pool graph cache in the fine-tuned-fusion path that hit 315 GB RSS on a supposedly tiny smoke test), a CUDA/fork deadlock in chemprop's DataLoader, and a Ray actor-pool leak that crashed RandomForest on its second training round. The headline result: MPN and a MoLFormer-based method that fine-tunes in-loop both reach ~95–98% of the true top-1000 at the largest batch size, our frozen fusion model reaches ~95% in under 11 minutes — two to three orders of magnitude faster than either — a follow-up that jointly fine-tunes two of the three backbones (GROVER + MoLFormer, UniMol kept frozen) reaches 97.3% at the largest batch size, matching MPN and MoLFormer-ft, once the memory footprint of doing that at 2.1M-molecule scale was brought under control — and simply replacing the frozen fusion model's fixed Borda-count combination with a learned RidgeCV meta-learner recovers most of that gap (96.7% vs. 95.0%) for the same few-minutes cost, no fine-tuning required. A later follow-up (§06), run with three seeds per configuration for real variance estimates, swaps in Uni-Mol2 (1.1B parameters, independently ported to standalone PyTorch and verified bit-exact against the real upstream implementation) for the frozen UniMol backbone: the fixed-weight Borda ensemble gains a real, seed-robust +2.0 to +3.3 points at the two smaller batch fractions, while the learned RidgeCV combiner's gains are smaller and mostly within seed noise — consistent with the learned combiner already partially compensating for backbone quality on its own.
01

Protocol

Reproduces the experimental setup of MolPAL's Enamine HTS Collection result (Graff, Shakhnovich & Coley, Chem. Sci. 2021, 12, 7866–7881, Figure 4, page 7876; arXiv:2012.07127). Pool and code provenance: github.com/coleygroup/molpal.

Docking scores are real AutoDock Vina results against thymidylate kinase (PDB 4UNN) sourced directly from the paper's own data release — nothing in this pipeline was redocked. Init size equals batch size, swept over three fractions of the scored pool (0.4%, 0.2%, 0.1%), with five exploration rounds. The reported metric is the fraction of the true top-1000 docking scores found, plotted against the number of molecules explored — matching the paper's own metric exactly, not the coarser top-1% that would apply at this pool size.

At the largest budget, MPN and MoLFormer-ft edge out the fusion model by 2–3 points (95.6–97.7% vs. 95.0%) — but the fusion model gets there in minutes, not hours: ~85× faster than MoLFormer-ft and ~190× faster than MPN at the 0.4% batch size. Swapping the frozen fusion model's fixed Borda-count combination for a RidgeCV linear meta-learner — same three frozen backbones, same few-minutes runtime, the only difference is how their predictions get combined — lifts recall at every batch size (96.7% vs. 95.0% at 0.4%; 89.6% vs. 87.0% at 0.2%; 79.4% vs. 72.2% at 0.1%). This replicates a pattern already seen in this project's earlier FusionAL benchmark (a different, smaller pool): a learned combination of frozen backbones consistently beats a fixed rank-based heuristic, and now confirmed at 2.1M-molecule scale too, not just the original smaller one.
04

ft_fusion: joint backbone fine-tuning

The frozen fusion model (§03) trades a couple of recall points for a ~100× speedup by never updating its backbones. This follow-up asks what happens if backbones do get fine-tuned jointly rather than frozen — the same in-loop fine-tuning MoLFormer-ft already does for one backbone, extended to two of the three. GROVER and MoLFormer are fine-tuned together each round from round 3 on (rounds 1–2 frozen, matching MoLFormer-ft's schedule) and the full 2.1M pool is re-embedded for both after every fine-tune step. UniMol is deliberately excluded from fine-tuning and stays frozen throughout — see the finding below for why. Greedy acquisition only, single seed, three batch fractions.

Fine-tuning two of the three backbones closes essentially all of the gap to MPN and MoLFormer-ft (97.3% vs. 95.6–97.7% at 0.4% batch) — confirming that the frozen fusion model's ~2–3 point deficit in §03 comes from never adapting its representations to the task, not from the fusion architecture itself. The cost is real, though: ft_fusion runs at MPN/MoLFormer-ft timescales (hours, not the frozen fusion model's minutes), since it now pays the same in-loop re-embedding cost MoLFormer-ft pays, twice over. Best score found (the pool's true optimum, −12.700 kcal/mol) was recovered by round 1 in every config.

05

Engineering findings

Eight independent bugs were found and fixed in the vendored MolPAL/chemprop stack while getting these runs to complete at full pool scale — none were exercised by the original codebase's own prior usage. Logged here because several were measured, not assumed, and two attempted optimizations were rejected after measurement showed they'd make things worse.

Unbounded chemprop graph cache — would have been ~15.3 TB at AmpC scaleRejected

Chemprop declares a global SMILES_TO_GRAPH cache with toggle functions but never actually wires it into batch_graph(). Wiring it up looked like a clean win — a molecule's graph representation never changes between AL rounds, only the model's weights do — but measured directly at 153.4 KB/molecule, which extrapolates to ~15.3 TB for a 99.5M-molecule pool. Reverted before it shipped.

Pre-existing chemprop RDKit-mol cache — ~1.77 TB at AmpC scaleFixed

Chemprop's CACHE_MOL=True default caches every parsed RDKit Mol object in a global dict, on by default, never disabled anywhere in this codebase. Measured at 17.8 KB/molecule → ~1.77 TB at 99.5M molecules. This was already active in every prior MPN run; disabled globally via set_cache_mol(False), verified correctness-preserving.

SLURM per-CPU memory cap silently overriding --exclusiveFixed

A fusion-mode job OOM'd on a confirmed 503 GB hopper node with a real memory footprint of only ~24–25 GB. --exclusive grants exclusive node access but doesn't by itself override a per-CPU default memory cap tied to --cpus-per-task. Fixed with an explicit --mem=0 on every submission script.

Unbatched full-pool prediction calls (MPN and fusion/MVE)Fixed

Two related bugs: chemprop's predict() built one MoleculeDataset for the entire ~2M-molecule remaining pool before any DataLoader batching happened, and the fusion/MVE prediction path did the equivalent for its embedding lookups. Both re-chunked to bounded 4,096–50,000-molecule slices at the call site, eliminating the multiplicative-copy memory blowup while leaving the underlying per-batch computation unchanged. Verified correctness-preserving via matched before/after smoke tests.

DataLoader fork+CUDA deadlock, worker parallelism measured counterproductiveFixed

Chemprop's MoleculeDataLoader only used the safer forkserver multiprocessing context off the main thread — but every real call path here runs on the main thread after CUDA is already initialized, the exact scenario that makes the default fork start method deadlock. Fixed to always use forkserver when workers are requested. Then tried actually enabling worker parallelism, expecting a speedup: measured ~2.4× slower (3,486.6s vs. 1,446.0s across two rounds on a 100K-molecule pool), most likely per-chunk worker startup/teardown overhead outweighing any gain. Reverted to zero workers; kept the deadlock fix as a dormant safety net.

RF crashing on its second training round via a Ray actor-pool leakFixed

RandomForestRegressor.fit()/.predict() were routed through joblib.parallel_backend("ray") — a fresh Ray-backed actor pool on every single call. Round 1 always succeeded; round 2 consistently crashed with a dead Ray actor, even under --exclusive/--mem=0 (ruling out a plain memory-cap explanation). Root cause: sklearn's own n_jobs=-1 already parallelizes RF natively via its default loky backend — the Ray indirection was unneeded complexity inherited from MolPAL's original distributed-cluster design. Removed entirely; verified with three consecutive rounds completing cleanly at consistent per-round timing.

ft_fusion's naive backbone caching hit ~315 GB RSS on a supposedly tiny local testFixed

GROVER's setup path eagerly built (or torch.load()ed a 51 GB on-disk cache of) one graph object per pool molecule, held resident for the finetuner's lifetime — and it ignored --pool-limit entirely, so even a "3,000-molecule" local smoke test silently loaded the real 2.1M-molecule cache and hit ~315 GB RSS before dying with zero output. Root cause traced, not guessed. Fix: measured GROVER graph construction from raw SMILES at ~3 ms/molecule (no conformers needed, 2D graph only) — cheap enough to build on demand per batch instead of caching the whole pool, so it's now rebuilt fresh for each fine-tune/extraction batch and discarded afterward. Separately, the three pre-extracted pool-embedding .npz files (used by every surrogate type, not just ft_fusion) were being fully decompressed and unconditionally copied on load; switched to memory-mapped .npy files, cutting peak load RSS from ~28–50 GB to ~1.85 GB, verified via direct measurement both before and after.

UniMol excluded from ft_fusion's joint fine-tuningFixed

Unlike GROVER, UniMol's embeddings come from a pre-generated, ragged conformer cache (10 ETKDG conformers + MMFF optimize per molecule) that can't be memory-mapped like a flat array, and can't be rebuilt on the fly either: conformer generation measured at ~400 ms/molecule — regenerating it for the full 2.1M pool would take ~2.4 days even with 4 parallel workers, exceeding a single round's budget by itself. Fine-tuning it the same way GROVER/MoLFormer are here would require holding its full-pool conformer cache resident in RAM (~300 GB+, the exact failure mode above). Resolved by keeping UniMol frozen throughout ft_fusion — it contributes its original, already memory-mapped embeddings unchanged every round, and never touches the risky code path. Verified with an integration test: UniMol's embeddings are byte-identical before and after two fine-tune rounds, and no UniMol finetuner is ever instantiated.

06

Uni-Mol2: swapping the frozen UniMol backbone

Uni-Mol2 (dptech/Uni-Mol2, 1.1B parameters) is a much larger, architecturally distinct successor to the UniMol backbone used in §03 — an AlphaFold-Evoformer-style two-track transformer (every layer updates both an atom representation and an explicit pairwise representation, not a one-shot 3D-distance bias). It shares no code with muben's UniMol implementation, so it was ported to standalone PyTorch from scratch this session and verified bit-exact against the real upstream Uni-Core-based implementation (checkpoint loaded into both, same input batch, max absolute difference 0.0, cosine similarity 1.0000 on every test molecule) before being trusted for anything downstream.

This section swaps Uni-Mol2 in for UniMol (backbones = GROVER + MoLFormer + Uni-Mol2) in both frozen-fusion surrogates from §03 — the fixed Borda-count Fusion and the RidgeCV learned_fusion — and compares against the §03 UniMol-based baseline. Same pool, same protocol, greedy acquisition, three batch fractions. Unlike §03–05, each configuration here was run with three seeds (both backbones, for a fair comparison) rather than one, specifically to give the comparison real variance estimates instead of single-run point estimates.

Methodological note: an initial pass at this comparison used a slightly wrong pool count to derive --init-size/--batch-size (2,141,500 raw SMILES rows, rather than the 2,104,318 molecules that actually have both an embedding and an oracle score), giving the Uni-Mol2 runs ~1.5–1.8% more labeled data per round than the UniMol baseline at the nominally same batch fraction. Caught before being reported as final; all numbers below use the corrected, exactly-matched batch sizes.

Wall time stayed in the same few-minutes range as §03's Fusion/learned_fusion (backbone size affects embedding-extraction cost, run once beforehand and not part of this AL-loop timing, far more than it affects the loop's own per-round MVE-head training/prediction cost).

The Uni-Mol2 swap helps the fixed-weight ensemble more than the learned one, and the effect is real (not seed noise) exactly where it's largest. Fusion's gains at 0.2% and 0.1% batch (+2.0 and +3.3 points) both clearly exceed the observed seed-to-seed spread on either side; its 0.4% gain (+0.5) does not. learned_fusion's gains are smaller and mostly within noise (+0.3, +1.3, +0.3, with the 0.2% figure sitting inside Uni-Mol2's own ±1.2% spread there). A plausible reading: Fusion combines backbones with fixed, unlearned weights, so a stronger backbone shows through directly, while learned_fusion fits its combination weights from data each round and so already partially compensates for whichever backbones it's given — leaving less room to gain from swapping one out. learned_fusion still beats Fusion outright at every batch size regardless of which UniMol variant is used, the most robust finding across all 36 runs in this section.
07

AmpC 99.5M: scaling the frozen-fusion model to a 47× larger pool

A first probe of the Fusion (ensemble) surrogate from §03 — same three frozen backbones (GROVER, MoLFormer, UniMol), same Borda rank-sum combination, same greedy acquisition, same 0.1% init/batch fraction and 5 rounds — run against AmpC β-lactamase (DOCK3.7 scores, sourced from Balius et al.'s Figshare release, not redocked here), a 99,459,561-molecule pool instead of Enamine HTS's 2.1M. Top-k is fixed at 50,000 rather than 1,000, keeping the two setups at the same percentile (≈0.05% of pool either way). Prediction over the full pool each round is delegated to a pool of 7 persistent single-GPU workers (chunked-prediction parallelization); surrogate training itself stays single-process, since the MVE heads are tiny relative to what dominates AL-loop cost at this scale.

One shard (12,432,439 molecules, ≈12.5% of the pool) is permanently excluded from candidate selection for this run — see Engineering notes below. Any true top-50,000 molecule inside that range can never be found here, which caps the achievable recall below 100% regardless of round count.

Compare against §03's closest analog — Fusion, greedy, 0.1% batch, 5 rounds, on the 2.1M pool: 72.2% recall in 5.7 minutes. Read this gap cautiously, not as "the method breaks at scale": both a 47× larger, more chemically diverse candidate pool and the 12.5% of the pool permanently excluded here would suppress recall independent of surrogate quality, and this run is a single seed, explicitly a first timing probe rather than a tuned campaign.

Engineering notes

Embedding files landed on a single, unstriped Lustre storage target. All three backbones' embedding arrays (593 GB grover, 285 GB molformer, 190 GB unimol) were written with the filesystem default stripe count of 1 — every scattered, fancy-indexed read (the AL loop's own labeled-set fetch, and one shard's per-chunk correction reads) funneled through one disk/controller regardless of how far apart the requested rows were. Confirmed I/O-bound rather than compute-bound via nvidia-smi showing 0% GPU utilization during a 2+ hour stall. Fixed by re-stitching all three from their existing per-chunk files into new 28-way-striped copies (pure I/O, no re-extraction; spot-verified bit-identical to the originals on a 2,000-row sample). One shard's per-round fetch continued to hang unpredictably even after striping (traced to ~192,000 within-shard duplicate SMILES breaking a contiguous-read optimization used elsewhere) and was excluded from this run rather than debugged further.

A specific compute node produced a reproducible CUDA "illegal instruction" crash during surrogate training. Happened identically, at the same call site, on two independent job submissions. Ruled out as a data or software issue by re-running the exact crashing 298,269-row batch (recovered from a snapshot written moments before each crash) on different GPU hardware with the same PyTorch/CUDA build — trained cleanly there. Both crashes traced to the same node via sacct; worked around with sbatch --exclude, not root-caused further.

08

Caveats & next steps

Single seed through §05. Every number in Sections 02–05 is one run, not a mean over repeats — small gaps between methods (e.g. 95.6% vs. 97.7% vs. 97.3% at the top of the pack) shouldn't be read as a confident ranking yet. §06 is the exception, run with three seeds specifically to address this for the Uni-Mol2 comparison.

RF's non-monotonic batch-size result (Section 02) reproduced identically on independent GPU and CPU hardware, so it isn't a fluke of one run — but it's still unexplained and worth understanding before it appears in anything citable.

ft_fusion is greedy-only so far (Section 04), matching the scope MoLFormer-ft and Fusion were first run at before UCB was added — a UCB sweep would let it join the full §03 comparison directly rather than sitting alongside it.

Uni-Mol2 fine-tuning support does not exist yet (Section 06) — the comparison there is frozen-backbone only, matching Fusion/learned_fusion's scope in §03, not ft_fusion's in §04.

AmpC (99.5M molecule, Figure 5) is now reported in §07, but as a single-seed first probe, not a tuned campaign — a 47× larger pool than everything above, with one shard (≈12.5% of it) permanently excluded from candidacy, and recall still climbing at the final round rather than converged. MPN's runtime at 99.5M remains an open problem — a naive linear extrapolation from the 2.1M numbers above puts a single MPN config at 2–3 weeks, and the two speed optimizations tried against that in §05 (graph caching, worker parallelism) were both measured and rejected, so only the Fusion surrogate has been attempted at this scale so far. Uni-Mol2 embeddings for the full 99.5M AmpC pool have not been computed.
